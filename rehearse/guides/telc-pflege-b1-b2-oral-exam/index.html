telc Deutsch B1·B2 Pflege: how the oral exam works and how to practise

How the speaking part of telc Deutsch B1·B2 Pflege is built, what the three tasks ask of you, and a simple routine to practise it out loud with others.

By the Rehearse team · Updated 2026-11-02

The speaking part of telc Deutsch B1·B2 Pflege is a pair exam: two candidates, three parts, about 16 minutes in total, after 20 minutes of preparation time. According to telc, the tasks are talking about experiences and opinions on a topic, discussing a controversial topic, and giving a short presentation. The written part covers listening, reading, language elements and a short nursing report. Below is how the exam is built and a practical way to prepare the speaking part, which is where many nurses feel least sure.

Before you book, ask the authority handling your recognition (Anerkennung), or your employer or agency, which language certificate they accept. Requirements differ between Länder and can change. The federal Recognition Portal helps you find who is responsible.

The exam at a glance

These are the figures telc publishes for the exam. Check the current exam description on telc.net before your date, because formats can be updated.

PartTasksTime
Listening (Hören)4 parts25 minutes
Reading (Lesen)4 parts60 minutes
Language elements (Sprachbausteine)2 partsnot listed separately
Writing (Schreiben)1 report, typical of nursing work30 minutes
Speaking (Sprechen)3 parts, pair examabout 16 minutes, plus 20 minutes preparation

telc also states that the exam cannot be taken as a partial examination, so you sit all parts together. The reading and listening texts come from nurses' everyday working life.

The speaking part, task by task

telc's own preparation booklet, Tipps zur Prüfungsvorbereitung (2017 edition), describes the speaking part in detail. You get 20 minutes to prepare the task sheets for parts 2 and 3, and you may take your notes into the exam room.

PartWhat happensAbout
1AYou describe a photo and say what kind of situation it shows (for example a ward round or an admission)1 min each
1BThe examiner names a topic and asks you follow-up questions1 min each
2AA short presentation on one of two topics, with keywords and three photos to help2 min each
2BThe examiner asks questions about your presentation2 min each
3A discussion with your partner on a set topic, with statements on the sheet you can pick up4 min

Two points from the booklet are worth knowing early. First, asking the examiner to repeat a question is not held against you; it is better than answering the wrong question. Second, the speaking part tests language, not whether your nursing content is correct.

Using the 20 minutes of preparation

  • Write keywords, not full sentences. Full sentences tempt you to read aloud, and reading sounds stiff.
  • For the presentation, pick the topic you have a real example for, and note at least two of the given points.
  • For the discussion, note one advantage, one disadvantage, one example from your work and one question for your partner.

Describing the photo and answering questions

Say what you see, then what situation it probably is. If you are not sure, say so: „Ich denke, das ist eine Visite.“ or „Vielleicht ist das die Aufnahme.“ For the follow-up questions, answer in full and bring in your own experience:

  • „In meinem Heimatland habe ich fünf Jahre auf einer chirurgischen Station gearbeitet.“
  • „Bei uns war es üblich, dass die Angehörigen bei der Pflege helfen.“
  • „Könnten Sie das bitte noch einmal sagen?“ when you did not catch the question.

The short presentation

The booklet recommends a clear structure: name the topic, say how your talk is built, cover the points, and end with your own opinion. In German:

  1. „Ich möchte über das Thema … sprechen.“
  2. „Ich spreche zuerst über …, dann über …“
  3. „Ein Beispiel aus meiner Arbeit: …“
  4. „Meiner Meinung nach …“

Practise at home with a timer: prepare a topic in about ten minutes, then speak for about two.

The discussion with your partner

The point is to talk with your partner: ask for their opinion, react to what they say, and ask back when you did not understand. Polite disagreement phrases help:

  • „Da stimme ich Ihnen zu, aber …“ I agree, but …
  • „Ich sehe das etwas anders, weil …“ I see it a little differently, because …
  • „Wie ist das bei Ihnen auf der Station?“ How is it on your ward?
  • „Das ist ein guter Punkt. Trotzdem finde ich …“ That's a good point. Still, I think …

Practise the language of the ward at the same time

The exam is one step; the first weeks on the ward are the next. The phrases that help in both are the same: handing over a patient, calling a doctor about a change, explaining something to a worried relative. A simple structure many nurses learn is SBAR (situation, background, assessment, recommendation). Practising one short handover a day in German, out loud, builds the same fluency the speaking part asks for:

  • „Ich rufe an wegen Herrn K. in Zimmer 4.“ (situation)
  • „Er ist gestern operiert worden.“ (background)
  • „Der Blutdruck ist seit einer Stunde gefallen.“ (assessment)
  • „Können Sie bitte vorbeikommen?“ (recommendation)

A simple four-week routine

  • Every day, 15 minutes: say one handover or one opinion out loud, recorded on your phone, then listen back once.
  • Twice a week: one discussion with a partner from your course, on a ward topic, with a timer.
  • Once a week: one full mock of the speaking part, with the 20-minute preparation and a partner asking follow-up questions you have not seen.
  • Use official materials: telc publishes the downloadable preparation booklet quoted above and a practice test, and publishers such as Klett sell exam-preparation books for this exam.

No routine can promise a result. What it does is make speaking feel familiar, so that on the day you can concentrate on what you want to say.
