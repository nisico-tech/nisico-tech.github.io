How to call the Kinderarzt in German and get an appointment

What to say when you phone a German paediatric practice: the opening lines, the questions you will hear, and what to say when you do not understand.

By the Rehearse team · Updated 2026-11-02

When you call a Kinderarzt practice, say who you are, which child it is, what is wrong and since when, then ask for an appointment today. Three short sentences are enough: „Guten Morgen, mein Name ist Ana Petrova. Mein Sohn ist drei Jahre alt und hat seit gestern Fieber. Haben Sie heute noch einen Termin frei?“ The person at the front desk (die Anmeldung) will then ask a few standard questions. If your answers to those are ready, the call is usually short and calm.

This guide is about language, not medical advice. If you think your child is in danger, call 112. Outside practice hours, the on-call medical service in Germany is 116117 (116117.de).

Before you dial: write four things down

Most calls go wrong because a question comes that you did not expect, not because of grammar. Put these on a piece of paper or in your notes:

  • The child: first name, surname and date of birth (das Geburtsdatum). Practices often find patients by date of birth.
  • The problem and since when: seit heute Morgen (since this morning), seit gestern Abend (since last night), seit drei Tagen (for three days).
  • Numbers you measured: a temperature is said with Komma: 39,2 is neununddreißig Komma zwei.
  • Whether your child is already a patient there. The question is „Ist Ihr Kind schon bei uns Patient?“

The call, step by step

StepYou sayMeaning
Greet and give your name„Guten Morgen, mein Name ist …“Good morning, my name is …
Say who it is about„Ich rufe wegen meines Sohnes an.“ / „… wegen meiner Tochter an.“I'm calling about my son / my daughter.
Say what is wrong„Er hat seit gestern Abend Fieber, 39 Grad.“He has had a fever since last night, 39 degrees.
Ask for the appointment„Können wir heute noch vorbeikommen?“Can we still come in today?
Repeat the time back„Also heute um halb zwölf. Vielen Dank.“So today at 11:30. Thank you.

Repeating the time back is the most useful habit on any German phone call. Halb zwölf means 11:30, not 12:30, and that small trap catches many people.

Questions you will probably hear

  • „Wie alt ist das Kind?“ How old is the child?
  • „Seit wann hat er Fieber?“ Since when has he had a fever?
  • „Wie hoch ist das Fieber?“ How high is the fever?
  • „Trinkt sie genug?“ Is she drinking enough?
  • „Hat er noch andere Beschwerden?“ Does he have any other symptoms?
  • „Wie ist Ihr Kind versichert?“ How is your child insured? The usual answers are gesetzlich (statutory) or privat.
  • „Bringen Sie bitte die Versichertenkarte mit.“ Please bring the insurance card.
  • „Kommen Sie in die offene Sprechstunde.“ Come to the open consultation hours (no fixed time; you wait your turn).

Some practices also ask you to bring the yellow check-up booklet, called das gelbe Heft or U-Heft. If you have it, put it in your bag with the insurance card.

When you do not understand

Asking again is normal, and receptionists hear it all day. These phrases buy you time without sounding rude:

  • „Entschuldigung, können Sie das bitte langsamer sagen?“ Sorry, could you say that more slowly?
  • „Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?“ Could you repeat that?
  • „Ich spreche noch nicht so gut Deutsch.“ My German isn't very good yet.
  • „Meinen Sie heute oder morgen?“ Do you mean today or tomorrow?
  • „Einen Moment, ich schreibe das kurz auf.“ One moment, I'll write that down.

If you freeze, a short honest sentence works better than silence: „Moment, ich muss kurz überlegen.“ (One moment, I need to think.)

If the practice is closed or full

Outside opening hours you will often hear a recorded message. Listen for two words: geschlossen (closed) and die Vertretung (the practice covering for this one). The message may give the covering practice's name and number; write it down and play the message again if you need to.

For illnesses that cannot wait until the practice opens again, Germany has the medical on-call service 116117. According to 116117.de, the line runs 24 hours a day, seven days a week, and is free from a German phone contract. The same site offers an online appointment service for people with statutory insurance, and it says clearly that 116117 is not for life-threatening emergencies: for those, call 112.

A useful first sentence for a 116117 call: „Mein Kind hat hohes Fieber und unsere Kinderarztpraxis ist geschlossen.“ (My child has a high fever and our paediatric practice is closed.)

Say it out loud once before the real call

Reading phrases silently and saying them are different skills. Before you call, say your three opening sentences out loud twice, at normal speed. Then ask a partner or friend to play the receptionist and ask you two of the questions above in a different order. It takes five minutes and it is the closest thing to the real call you can get without making it.

Keep a small card with your opening line, your child's date of birth and the phrase „Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?“. On the phone, a card in front of you is allowed and it helps.
