How many questions do you need to pass the driving theory test?

Car theory test pass marks: 43 of 50 questions and 44 of 75 in hazard perception. How both parts work, and how to check you are really ready.

By the Honest Theory team · Updated 2026-11-02

To pass the car theory test in Great Britain you need 43 out of 50 in the multiple-choice part and 44 out of 75 in the hazard perception part, and you must pass both parts in the same test (GOV.UK). You have 57 minutes for the 50 questions. The hazard perception part has 14 video clips. This guide explains how each part is scored, what the official rules say about clicking, and how to tell if you are ready before you book.

Sources: multiple-choice questions, hazard perception test and pass mark and result on GOV.UK. The car theory test costs £23 (GOV.UK), you need a provisional licence to book it, and you get the result at the test centre. A pass certificate number lasts for 2 years (GOV.UK).

Part 1: the 50 multiple-choice questions

You answer on a computer. Before the test starts you can do a practice question to get used to the screens, and you can finish early once you have answered everything (GOV.UK). Three questions are about a short, silent video that you can watch as many times as you like.

43 out of 50 means you can drop at most 7 marks. That is a smaller margin than it sounds. Many marks are lost not because people do not know the rule, but because they misread the question:

  • “NOT” and “LEAST” questions ask for the wrong or worst option. If you read too fast, you pick the right behaviour, which is the wrong answer.
  • “Mark two answers” questions need both answers.
  • “MOST” questions often have several reasonable options; only one is the best.

A useful habit: read the last line of the question first and say the key word to yourself before you look at the options.

Part 2: hazard perception

You watch 14 clips. Each one shows at least one developing hazard, and one clip shows two (GOV.UK). You can score up to 5 points for each developing hazard: the earlier you respond once it starts to develop, the more you score. That makes 15 hazards and a maximum of 75 points.

Three official rules matter a lot:

  • Clicking in a pattern or continuously scores nothing for that clip (GOV.UK).
  • Clicking at the wrong moment does not cost you points. So one or two extra clicks as a hazard develops are fine; a rhythm is not.
  • You get one attempt per clip and cannot review or change your responses.
Practise the timing, not just the spotting. A hazard you notice too late still scores low, and a clip where you click non-stop scores zero.

What revision material does DVSA point to?

GOV.UK lists The Highway Code, Know your traffic signs, Driving – the essential skills and the official DVSA theory test book and apps, plus a free practice theory test (GOV.UK). It also says the practice questions are not used in the real test, but are based on the same topics. That last point matters: what you need is understanding, not a memorised list.

If reading is hard for you

You can ask, when you book, to hear the test through headphones (English or Welsh), and you can ask for more time for the multiple-choice part. Other support, such as someone reading the screen to you, needs proof of your reading difficulty (GOV.UK). Ask for it at booking; it is easier than asking later.

How to know you are really ready

The most common trap is practising the same questions until you score 50 out of 50. By then you are recognising questions, not reading them. A fairer check:

  1. Test yourself on questions you have not seen recently. If your score drops a lot on new questions, you are remembering, not understanding.
  2. Aim above the line. Scoring exactly 43 in practice leaves no room for a bad day. Look for a few marks of margin on more than one fresh mock.
  3. Check every topic. A good total can hide one weak topic.
  4. Treat hazard perception separately. Passing the questions does not cover it, and on the day you must pass both.
  5. Move the date if you need to. You can change your test if you give at least 3 full working days’ notice; with less notice you pay again (GOV.UK).

On the day

Use the practice question at the start to settle in, read every question to its last word, and flag anything you are unsure of so you can come back to it within the 57 minutes. In the hazard part, click once when you see a hazard start to develop and once more as it develops, then stop.
