Failed your theory test? When you can retake it and what to change

Failed the driving theory test? You can retake it after 3 working days, but you sit both parts again. What the rules say, and how to find what cost you marks.

By the Honest Theory team · Updated 2026-11-02

If you failed your car theory test, you have to wait at least 3 working days before you take it again, and you must book and take the full test again, even if you passed one of the two parts this time (GOV.UK). Each attempt costs £23 (GOV.UK). Failing is common: in July to September 2024, the car theory test pass rate was 45.7% (Department for Transport). The useful question is not “how soon can I rebook?” but “what exactly cost me the marks?”.

The rules after a fail

QuestionAnswer
How soon can I retake?At least 3 working days after the test
Do I redo both parts?Yes, the full test, even if you passed one part
How much does it cost?£23 for a car theory test
What are the pass marks?43 out of 50 questions; 44 out of 75 hazard perception
Can I move a booked test?Yes, with at least 3 full working days’ notice, or you pay again

Sources: pass mark and result, book your theory test and change your theory test on GOV.UK.

Step 1: write down your scores today

Note your score for each part while you still remember how the test felt:

  • Questions below 43: how many short were you? One or two marks is a few fixes, not the whole book.
  • Hazard perception below 44: did you feel late on the hazards, or did you click a lot? GOV.UK says clicking continuously or in a pattern scores nothing for that clip (source).
  • Both below: start with the part that was further from its pass mark.

Step 2: find out what kind of mistakes you made

Lost marks on the questions usually come in two kinds, and they need different fixes.

Rule gaps

You did not know the rule: stopping distances, motorway rules, who has priority, what a sign means. The fix is the source material. GOV.UK points to The Highway Code, Know your traffic signs and Driving – the essential skills (GOV.UK). Study one topic at a time, then test yourself on that topic with questions you have not seen.

Wording slips

You knew the rule but the question tripped you: a “NOT” you read past, a “MOST” where two options seemed right, or a “Mark two answers” where you picked one. The fix is a reading habit, not more rules:

  1. Read the question stem before the options.
  2. Say the key word to yourself: NOT, LEAST, MOST, two answers.
  3. For NOT questions, find the right behaviour first, then pick the option that breaks it.

Step 3: fix hazard perception on its own

You score up to 5 points per developing hazard, with 14 clips and 15 hazards; there is one attempt per clip and no review (GOV.UK). Clicking at the wrong time does not cost points, but a rhythm or non-stop clicking scores zero. Practise noticing the moment a hazard starts to develop: a car indicating, a pedestrian stepping towards the kerb, a cyclist looking over a shoulder. One click as you notice it, one more as it develops.

Step 4: stop counting practice scores you have memorised

After a fail it is tempting to redo the same practice tests until you score 50 out of 50. GOV.UK notes that its practice questions are not used in the real test, though they cover the same topics (source). If you have seen a question several times, getting it right shows memory. Before you book again, check yourself on questions you have not seen recently, under real timing (50 questions, 57 minutes, per GOV.UK), more than once.

Rebook when your fresh-question results are a few marks above 43 on more than one mock, not when the old practice test finally says 50.

If reading was the problem

If you struggled to read the questions in time, you can ask for headphones with audio (English or Welsh) and for extra time on the multiple-choice part when you book; some support needs proof of a reading difficulty (GOV.UK).

A simple two-week plan (example)

  • Days 1-3: the topics where you lost most marks, one per day, then 10 fresh questions on each.
  • Days 4-7: 10 minutes a day on tricky wording (NOT, LEAST, MOST, two answers), plus a few hazard clips.
  • Days 8-12: mixed sessions on questions you have not seen recently, and one full timed mock.
  • Days 13-14: a second full mock. If both are a few marks clear of 43 and your hazard practice is clear of 44, book.

Adjust it to your own gaps. If your hazard score was the problem, swap most of the question time for clips.

Be kind about it

With fewer than half of car theory tests passed in the quarter above (DfT), failing says little about whether you will be a good driver. It does tell you where to look.
