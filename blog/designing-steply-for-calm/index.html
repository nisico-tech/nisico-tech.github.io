Designing Steply to be calm

NISICO studio · 2026-11-04

Steply is a visual schedule app for families of autistic children ages 3 to 7. Many choices in it come from one question: what would make this screen calmer for a child who finds the unexpected hard?

One step at a time

In child mode, the screen shows one big picture and one Done button. The steps ahead are small dots at the top, so the child can see how much is left without reading a list. A big step can split into small ones: "get dressed" becomes "left sock", then "right sock".

No surprises on the child's screen

No ads, no sudden sounds, no flashing, no countdowns that rush. Rewards are quiet: a calm All done and a star, with no streak to lose and no red marks for a step that did not happen.

The parent stays in control

The parent's Today screen shows the next routine in one big card, ready to hand over. A parent lock keeps everything else closed. Pictures can be swapped for photos of the child's own things, because a real toothbrush is often easier to recognize than an icon.

Help for when the plan breaks

Plans change: rain, a closed park, a late bus. Help now is one tap away with three tools: Breathe together, a slow circle to breathe with; First / Then, two pictures in seconds; and Change of plan, which swaps a routine or resets the rest of today while keeping the usual routines for tomorrow.

What it is not

Steply is a visual support and planning tool. It is not therapy and does not replace a child's doctor, therapist or teacher. We say that on every page, because parents deserve to know exactly what they are getting.
