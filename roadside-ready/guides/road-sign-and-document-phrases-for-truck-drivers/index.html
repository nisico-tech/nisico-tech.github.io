Road sign and document phrases truck drivers need in English

Plain English phrases for your CDL, logs, bill of lading and load, plus how to explain road signs in your own words for the roadside English check.

By the Roadside Ready team · Updated 2026-11-02

For the roadside English check you need two kinds of English: short answers about your trip, documents and truck, and the ability to explain what a road sign means. You do not need perfect grammar. You need clear, short sentences you can say without stopping. Below are practical phrases and a simple method for explaining signs, based on what inspectors ask according to official guidance.

Why these phrases

When an inspector starts an inspection, the usual first questions are where you are coming from, where you are going, what company you work for, your CDL and what you are hauling (Oregon DOT safety notice). FMCSA’s 2025 guidance, as reported by Trucking Dive, adds topics like your time on duty, your logbook and driving time, your shipping papers and the truck’s equipment. If the formal assessment goes to road signs, the inspector picks four signs and you must explain at least three (Oregon DOT).

Phrases about your trip

The inspector may askA clear answer (sample)
Where are you coming from?I am coming from Laredo, Texas.
Where are you going?I am going to Atlanta, Georgia.
What company do you work for?I work for Sample Freight Lines.
What are you hauling?I am hauling auto parts. / I am empty.
How long have you been driving today?I started at six this morning. I have driven about five hours.
When did you last take a break?I took a thirty-minute break at noon.

Use your own real facts. The names above are samples. The point is the shape of the sentence: start with “I am…” or “I work…”, then one fact.

Phrases about your documents

Know the English name of each document you carry and one sentence for each:

  • CDL (commercial driver’s license): “Here is my CDL.”
  • Medical card (medical examiner’s certificate): “Here is my medical card.”
  • Logs / ELD (electronic logging device): “My logs are on the ELD. I can show you on the screen.”
  • Bill of lading (the shipping papers for your load): “Here is the bill of lading.”
  • Registration and insurance: “The registration is in this folder.”
  • Annual inspection sticker or report: “The annual inspection is here.”

Practise handing over a document and saying the sentence at the same time. It sounds simple, but under pressure people often forget the English word for something they use every day.

Phrases about the truck and equipment

  • “The truck is a 2021 tractor with a 53-foot dry van.” (sample)
  • “I did my pre-trip inspection this morning.”
  • “The brakes, lights and tires were OK.”
  • “I have a fire extinguisher and warning triangles.”

Phrases when you do not understand

Asking is normal English, not a mistake. The Oregon procedure tells inspectors to rephrase if the driver struggles and to give the driver time to reply (source).

  • “Can you repeat that, please?”
  • “Can you say it slower, please?”
  • “Sorry, I don’t understand the word ‘…’.”

How to explain a road sign

US road signs follow one national manual, the MUTCD, published by the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA). The check includes changeable (electronic) message signs as well as regular ones (Oregon DOT). You are explaining what the sign means, so answer in a simple pattern:

  1. Say what it is. “This is a stop sign.” / “This sign says road closed ahead.”
  2. Say what you must do. “I must stop completely.” / “I must take another road.”
  3. Add one detail if you can. “Then I go when it is safe.” / “I will follow the detour signs.”

Read each sign out loud, cover the answer, and explain it in your own words. Then say it again shorter. Short and correct is better than long and unsure.

Practise before you drive, never during. Phones, apps, interpreters and cue cards are not allowed during the assessment itself (Trucking Dive).

A 10-minute daily routine

  • 2 minutes: say your trip facts for today out loud.
  • 3 minutes: name five documents and say the sentence for each.
  • 3 minutes: explain four signs, one sentence each.
  • 2 minutes: ask a friend or family member to ask you two questions in English, and answer without notes.
