How to block porn on your iPhone for free (and what Screen Time can't do)

How to turn on the free adult-website filter and app schedules in iPhone Screen Time, make them harder to undo, and what no iPhone blocker can do.

By the Stillwater team · Updated 2026-11-02

Short answer: your iPhone already has a free porn filter. Go to Settings > Screen Time > Content & Privacy Restrictions, turn restrictions on, then open App Store, Media, Web & Games > Web Content and choose Limit Adult Websites (Apple: Use Screen Time on iPhone). Add a Screen Time passcode so the setting cannot be changed on impulse. It will catch a lot, not everything, and you can always switch it off in Settings, so treat it as a speed bump that buys you time, not a wall.

Last checked against Apple's iPhone User Guide in November 2026 (iOS 26 and iOS 27). Menu names move between iOS versions. The steps below follow Apple's iOS 26 guide; on newer versions look for the same words under Screen Time. If something looks different, Apple's own pages linked here are the reference.

Step 1: turn on the adult-website filter

  1. Open Settings and tap Screen Time. If you have never used it, turn it on for yourself first (Apple: Set up Screen Time for yourself).
  2. Tap Content & Privacy Restrictions and switch it on.
  3. Tap App Store, Media, Web & Games, then Web Content.
  4. Choose Limit Adult Websites.
  5. Under Never Allow, add any specific sites you know are a problem for you.

This filter works in Safari and in other browsers that support Apple's content filtering. Apple turns it on by default for child accounts under 18 (Apple: Set up parental controls); as an adult, you are simply choosing the same protection for yourself.

Step 2: make it harder to undo at 1 a.m.

A filter you can turn off in ten seconds will be turned off in the moment you most want it on. Without a Screen Time passcode, an app limit offers an "Ignore Limit" button that takes one tap; with a passcode, getting past it needs the code. Two ways to add a delay:

  • Set a Screen Time passcode (Apple: Create, change or remove a Screen Time passcode). Choose a code you will not remember easily. Some people write it down and put it in a drawer in another room, so changing the setting means getting up.
  • Ask someone you trust to set the passcode. A partner, friend or mentor types in a code you don't know. This is a bigger step: it works best when it is your choice, the other person knows it is a safety net and not a surveillance tool, and you both agree on how you can ask for it.

Be aware that Apple lets you reset a forgotten Screen Time passcode with your Apple Account if you set up passcode recovery, so even this is a delay, not a lock.

Step 3: put the gateway apps on a schedule

For many people, the browser is not where it starts. It starts with scrolling a social or video app late at night, and porn is two or three taps later. Screen Time can limit those apps too:

  • On recent iOS versions, use Screen Time Schedule and Time Allowances to decide when categories like Social Media are available (Apple: Set schedules and time allowances).
  • On older versions, the same idea lives under Downtime and App Limits.

Pick your hard hours (for example 23:00 to 07:00) and limit the two or three apps where the loop usually starts. Keep the calls, messages and apps you actually need on the always-allowed list.

What Screen Time can't do

It helps to know the limits before you rely on it, so a gap doesn't feel like failure:

  • It can be switched off. Whoever knows the passcode, or controls the Apple Account, can turn restrictions off. No iPhone app can stop the owner of the phone from changing their own settings, and an app is not reliably told when you do. Be wary of any product that calls itself "unbreakable".
  • The filter is not perfect. Automatic filters miss some sites and occasionally block harmless ones. Explicit content also shows up inside social apps, where a website filter can't see it. That is why limiting the gateway apps in your hard hours matters.
  • It doesn't deal with the urge. Blocking buys you a minute. What you do with that minute (a plan, a reason, a change of room) is what makes the difference over time. Our guide to stopping at night covers this.

Paid blockers and accountability apps: what to check

There are many paid apps that add more blocking or send reports to a partner. Some people find them useful. Before you pay, check:

  • What is sent, and to whom. Some accountability apps take screenshots of your screen or send lists of sites to another person. Decide whether you are comfortable with that, and whether your partner actually wants to receive it.
  • Where your data goes. Data about your sexual behaviour is about as sensitive as it gets. In 2026, a popular quit-porn app was reported to have exposed intimate data of hundreds of thousands of users through a misconfigured database (404 Media). Prefer apps that keep your data on the phone and do not require an account.
  • How billing works. Look for a clearly stated price and renewal date, a reminder before a trial ends, and an easy way to cancel.

A simple setup for tonight

  1. Turn on Limit Adult Websites.
  2. Set a Screen Time passcode you won't remember easily.
  3. Put your two or three gateway apps on a schedule for your hard hours.
  4. Charge the phone outside the bedroom if you can.
  5. Write down one sentence about why you are doing this, and keep it where you will see it.

That is a free, private setup that takes about ten minutes.
