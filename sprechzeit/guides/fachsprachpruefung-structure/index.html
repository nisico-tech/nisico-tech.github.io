What is the Fachsprachprüfung (FSP) for doctors and how is it structured?

The German FSP for doctors explained: who needs it, how you get a date, what happens in each of the three 20-minute parts, and what varies by Land.

By the Sprechzeit team · Updated 2026-11-02

The Fachsprachprüfung (FSP) is the German medical language exam that doctors with a foreign degree usually need before they can get a licence to practise (Approbation) or a temporary permit (Berufserlaubnis). It tests medical German oriented at level C1, not your medical knowledge. In most Länder it is a one-to-one exam of about one hour in three parts of 20 minutes each: a conversation with a simulated patient, a written documentation of that case, and a conversation with a doctor about the patient. The details (fees, examiners, permitted aids) are set by each Land, so always check the information sheet of the body that will examine you.

This guide summarises public information from two state bodies and a trade article. It is not legal advice. Your Land's authority and Ärztekammer decide what applies to you.

Where the FSP comes from

Doctors licensed in Germany must have the German language skills their work requires. In June 2014 the 87th conference of the German health ministers (Gesundheitsministerkonferenz) agreed key points for a common procedure: doctors need specialist language skills in a professional context oriented at level C1, normally proven by a Fachsprachprüfung. The Niedersachsen approbation office describes this on its FSP information sheet (as of 1 January 2026).

To be admitted, you also need a certificate of general German at least at level B2, as the Ärztekammer Westfalen-Lippe and Deutsches Ärzteblatt's jobs portal both explain.

How you get to the exam

You usually do not book the FSP yourself. You apply for the Approbation or Berufserlaubnis with the authority responsible in your Land. Once your file is complete, that authority registers you with the Ärztekammer (or the body that runs the exam there), and you are asked to pay the fee. Then you receive a date. Both the Niedersachsen sheet and the Westfalen-Lippe leaflet describe this order.

The three parts

PartWhat you doTime
1. Arzt-Patienten-GesprächTake a history from a simulated patient, explain the suspected diagnosis and answer the patient's questions20 min
2. DokumentationWrite up the case for a colleague (an Arztbericht), including the next steps20 min
3. Arzt-Arzt-GesprächPresent the patient to a doctor on the panel, then answer questions20 min

Part 1: the patient conversation

In Westfalen-Lippe a doctor on the panel plays the patient. You record the personal details, the current complaints, previous illnesses, medication and family history, then tell the patient your suspected diagnoses and respond to their questions. The leaflet stresses language the patient can understand, with as few foreign words and technical terms as possible. You may take notes; they are not marked, but you hand them in at the end (ÄKWL).

The Niedersachsen sheet sets the bar this way: you must understand the patient without frequent questions back, take a careful history, explain findings and the planned course, and discuss the pros and cons of a measure and its alternatives without obviously searching for words (NiZzA).

Part 2: the documentation

You summarise the medically relevant information from the conversation in a report addressed to a colleague and describe how to proceed. In Westfalen-Lippe you may use your notes from Part 1, a report form is provided, a PC is available, and a medical dictionary may be used; where the form asks for it, you write in full sentences (ÄKWL). Other chambers may handle aids differently.

Part 3: the doctor-to-doctor conversation

You hand over what you learned to a doctor on the panel, concisely and with correct medical terms, then answer their questions. In Westfalen-Lippe the part ends with a short list of common medical terms for which you give the everyday German word (ÄKWL). The Niedersachsen sheet explains the aim: being clear enough with colleagues that misunderstandings, and the wrong diagnoses or treatments they could cause, are ruled out (NiZzA).

What differs between Länder

The structure above is shared, but practical details are not. Two examples from official sheets:

Niedersachsen (NiZzA, as of 01.01.2026)Westfalen-Lippe (ÄKWL, as of 15.02.2021)
ExaminersAt least two, at least one of them a doctorA panel of three, at least two of them licensed doctors
Fee€595€350
Medical knowledgeNot testedOnly language aspects are assessed
RetakesWhole exam repeated; number of attempts not limitedAs often as needed; the chamber recommends a gap of 3 to 6 months

The Ärzteblatt jobs portal puts fees across Länder at roughly €350 to €600 and notes that each new attempt is paid again (aerztestellen.aerzteblatt.de). The doctors' union Marburger Bund has published a fee overview by Land; it dates from 2019, so check current figures with your authority. In Westfalen-Lippe, phones, bags and other aids may not be taken into the exam rooms.

The FSP and the Kenntnisprüfung are different exams

The FSP checks language. If your training is not found equivalent to German training, you may also have to sit the Kenntnisprüfung, a separate exam of medical knowledge. Whether you need it is decided in your recognition procedure; ask your authority, not a forum.

How to prepare, in practice

  • Get your own Land's sheet. Search for your Ärztekammer or approbation office plus “Fachsprachprüfung” and read the current information sheet. It is the only source that counts.
  • Train all three parts as one chain. The documentation and the presentation depend on how well you took the history. Practise the same case from start to finish.
  • Practise out loud with a timer. Twenty minutes feels long when you read and very short when you speak.
  • Practise switching registers. Plain words for the patient („Haben Sie Wasser in den Beinen?“), technical terms for the colleague (Beinödeme).
  • Use a course if you can. Language courses and supervised hospital placements (Hospitationen) are what the Westfalen-Lippe leaflet recommends before a retake.
